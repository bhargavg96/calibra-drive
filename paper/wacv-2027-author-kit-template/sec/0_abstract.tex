\begin{abstract}
World models for autonomous driving are increasingly used for planning and simulation, yet their predictive uncertainty remains poorly understood. While these models can generate realistic future states, overconfident or poorly calibrated predictions can lead to catastrophic downstream planning failures. To address this gap, we introduce CalibraDrive, the first systematic benchmark for evaluating the calibration of driving world models. We evaluate representative architectures, including 3D occupancy (OccWorld) and video generation (Vista) models, across spatial dimensions, temporal horizons, and scenario difficulties on the nuScenes dataset. Our findings indicate that all tested models are significantly overconfident (\ece~> \TODO{0.05}), with calibration degrading further over longer prediction horizons and at greater distances from the ego vehicle. Furthermore, we demonstrate that simple post-hoc recalibration methods can reduce the \ece~by over \TODO{50\%}. Importantly, integrating calibrated uncertainty measurably improves downstream planning safety, highlighting the critical need for uncertainty-aware world modeling in autonomous driving.
\end{abstract}
