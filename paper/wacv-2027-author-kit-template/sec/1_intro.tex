\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

World models for autonomous driving are transforming the landscape of vehicle planning and simulation \cite{zheng2024occworld, gao2024vista, hu2023gaia1, wang2023drivedreamer, yang2023unisim}. By learning a predictive distribution over future driving states conditioned on current context and ego actions, these generative models offer an elegant, scalable alternative to hand-crafted simulation environments. However, while world models have demonstrated impressive capability in generating realistic multi-modal futures, a critical question remains largely unanswered: \textit{do we know when these models are wrong?} 

When a driving world model makes an overconfident but incorrect prediction about the future trajectory of a pedestrian or the occupancy of an intersection, downstream planning algorithms that rely on these predictions can make unsafe, potentially catastrophic decisions. Predictive uncertainty is essential for safe autonomous driving \cite{mei2025c3}. In practice, an autonomous vehicle should act cautiously when its predictive model is uncertain, prioritizing safety over progress. But if the world model itself produces poorly calibrated probabilities---predicting high confidence for incorrect futures---the planner is effectively flying blind.

Calibration has been extensively studied in standard deep learning tasks like image classification and object detection \cite{guo2017calibration}, yet it remains largely unexplored for generative driving world models. Existing literature on uncertainty in autonomous driving primarily focuses on perception-level uncertainty or traditional prediction models, lacking a systematic evaluation of modern large-scale world models. While recent methods like C3 \cite{mei2025c3} and RELIOCC have proposed specific calibration techniques for certain prediction tasks, there currently exists no standardized benchmark for evaluating and comparing the calibration of diverse driving world model architectures.

In this paper, we present CalibraDrive, the first systematic benchmark for evaluating the predictive uncertainty and calibration of driving world models. Our framework spans diverse representative architectures, evaluating both 3D occupancy forecasting (OccWorld \cite{zheng2024occworld}) and video generation (Vista \cite{gao2024vista}) paradigms. Through extensive spatial-temporal analysis, we reveal where and when these models fail, demonstrating that confidence is poorly aligned with accuracy, particularly at longer prediction horizons and in hard driving scenarios.

In summary, our key contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{CalibraDrive Benchmark:} We introduce the first comprehensive framework for evaluating calibration in generative driving world models across different architectures (occupancy and video).
    \item \textbf{Spatial-Temporal Calibration Analysis:} We provide an in-depth analysis of where and when driving world models are miscalibrated, showing degradation over prediction horizons and spatial distances.
    \item \textbf{Recalibration and Downstream Safety:} We demonstrate that simple post-hoc recalibration techniques not only significantly improve calibration metrics (\eg, reducing Expected Calibration Error) but also measurably enhance the safety of downstream planning algorithms.
\end{enumerate}
