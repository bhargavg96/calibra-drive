\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Driving World Models.}
The success of large-scale generative models has spurred significant interest in developing world models for autonomous driving. These models generally predict future states conditioned on historical context and ego-vehicle actions, enabling both closed-loop simulation and model-based planning. OccWorld \cite{zheng2024occworld} predicts future 3D occupancy and ego trajectories autoregressively. Video-based world models such as Vista \cite{gao2024vista}, GAIA-1 \cite{hu2023gaia1}, and DriveDreamer \cite{wang2023drivedreamer} formulate the problem as future video generation. Other notable works include UniSim \cite{yang2023unisim}, GenAD \cite{zheng2024genad}, Copilot4D \cite{zhang2024copilot4d}, and Drive-WM \cite{wang2024drivewm}. While these models achieve impressive fidelity in predicting future states, their predictive confidence and uncertainty have not been rigorously evaluated.

\paragraph{Calibration in Deep Learning.}
Calibration measures the alignment between a model's predicted confidence and its actual empirical accuracy. Deep neural networks, particularly large modern architectures, are often notoriously overconfident \cite{guo2017calibration}. Various methods have been proposed to improve calibration, ranging from post-hoc scaling methods like Platt scaling \cite{platt1999probabilistic} and temperature scaling \cite{guo2017calibration} to distribution-free guarantees using conformal prediction \cite{vovk2005algorithmic, romano2019conformal}. Other prominent approaches include Bayesian Neural Networks, MC Dropout \cite{gal2016dropout}, and Deep Ensembles \cite{lakshminarayanan2017simple}. While these techniques are well-established for classification and regression, adapting and evaluating them for high-dimensional, generative driving world models remains an open challenge.

\paragraph{Uncertainty in Autonomous Driving.}
Uncertainty estimation is critical for safe autonomous driving. While extensive research has addressed uncertainty in 3D object detection, semantic segmentation, and traditional trajectory prediction, exploring uncertainty in comprehensive driving world models is a nascent field. The most closely related work to ours is C3 \cite{mei2025c3}, which explores calibration for conformal trajectory prediction, and RELIOCC, which targets occupancy calibration. However, these works primarily propose specific methodologies for specific tasks. In contrast, CalibraDrive provides a standardized \textit{benchmark} and systemic evaluation across fundamentally different world model architectures (\eg, 3D occupancy vs. video generation), temporal horizons, and planning settings.
